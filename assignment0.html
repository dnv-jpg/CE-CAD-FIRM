Assignment 0 – Introduction to Engineering Project Management

IE492 Spring 2026 – Dr. Ranky

Introduction

This assignment introduces engineering project management through six video case studies covering real manufacturing and automation projects. For each video the goal is to analyze the engineering problem shown, understand the existing solutions, and think about how a better solution could be designed, tested, and validated. That is the actual analytical work. It is not about just describing what the videos show. The second part goes over D&V Civil Engineering Management and how the company was set up, its sustainable green design focus, and how it plans to collaborate with four other companies to deliver engineering projects. The EM-1 materials covered in this course provide the framework for everything here including goal setting, team organization, prioritization, and flexibility in project management.

Video 1: Bentley Quality Design

Video 1 talks about the design and engineering ideas in the Bentley Continental GT in an interview with John Crawford, the public relations director for Bentley Motors North America. The main engineering issue talked about in the video was how Bentley kept producing luxury cars and mastered the design and performance side. Crawford talked about how the high demand for Continental GT, and how it was the best in the market. It had an MSRP of $238,000 and still had a long waiting list. Crawford explained that Bentley focuses on combining performance, luxury, and exclusivity while still keeping the brand's identity strong. Bentley is defined by its design, performance and the prestige that comes with owning one. Crawford also talked about how the quality and craftsmanship is important in Bentley. The video also talks about how manufacturers inspect and verify quality during production. Traditional methods like manual inspections, standard tolerance checks, and CNC machining are commonly used in automotive manufacturing, but luxury vehicles need higher standard production rules. One improvement that can help would be using automated vision inspection systems during different stages of assembly and with these systems it will help find defects earlier. In D&V this can help by directly cordinating quality is verified in BIM projects. The same principle of catching problems as early as possible rather than at submission.

Video 2: Chemical Engineering Process (Siemens)

Video 2 talks about the importance of automation and monitoring technology in chemical engineering. It interviews Vincent Turmel, PHD Systems Specialist at Process Automation Systems, Siemens Energy and Automation inc. The main engineering issue talked about in the video is how chemical plants monitor machines and safety systems so production runs efficiently. Turmel explained that automation systems help engineers see real time data like pressure, flow, and temperature through a control interface. The control interface helps detect problems early and avoid shutdowns and safety risks. The system also uses controllers that make communication faster and more reliable compared to traditional systems. In the video it also talks about how many manufacturing systems still use batch processing, manual control adjustments, programmable logic controllers, and quality testing after production is completed. While these methods work, batch systems create variation between production runs and manual adjustments and increases the risk of human error. One solution that could help would be using continuous process monitoring with automated feedback control systems that adjust process variables in real time. This would reduce variation and identify problems earlier before the final product. Testing and validation could involve comparing product quality between automated systems and manual control systems during production runs. In D&V this would help how project performance is monitored and how to find problems eariler on.

Video 3: Cognex Vision Inspection

Video 3 talks about a manufacturing engineering project that uses machine vision inspection systems to improve quality control. The goal is to automatically inspect products on the production line by detecting defects, verifying features, and reading codes before deciding if a part is acceptable or not. The main engineering issue talked about in the video is how to maintain high inspection accuracy while still keeping production fast and efficient. Since manufacturing lines move fast now the system has to detect real errors. The video also talks about how many companies still use manual visual inspections, simple photo sensors, and basic dimensional measurements during production which work but manual inspections can be inconsistent. Also simple sensors can also only detect basic information like whether a part is present or missing. One improvement that could help would be using camera based vision systems with image processing software that can classify defects by type and severity. These systems can help identify defects more accurately while reducing unnecessary part rejection. Testing and validation could consistent of measuring the true positive rate, false rejection rate, and miss rate to compare automated inspections with manual inspections on the same production batch. The Cognex system is better than relying only on manual inspections because it can inspect products at full production speed while applying the same inspection standards to every part. In D&V tthis can help to show how model checking software can identify BIM coordination problems before a final review is completed.

Video 4: FANUC Fixtureless Robotic Assembly

Video 4 talks about an advanced robotics project focused on fixtureless assembly using FANUC robotic systems. It is an industrial engineering project where the main goal is to eliminate traditional mechanical fixtures and instead rely on robotic intelligence to position and assemble parts correctly. Instead of using fixed fixtures to hold components in place, the robots use sensors, programming, and precise motion control to complete the assembly process. The main engineering issue talked about in the video was how to maintain high precision and consistency without using the rigid support structures that are normally used in manufacturing. In the video it also talks about how many manufacturing systems still use dedicated fixtures and jigs for each product type, manual assembly for more flexible operations, and robotic systems programmed for only one specific task which work but are expensive to build. One soultion that could help would be using adaptive robotic gripping systems and vision sensors that can identify part orientation and placement without needing fixed fixtures. Testing and validation could involve running the robotic system through different product variations while measuring repeatability, cycle time consistency, and defect rates during assembly. In D&V this will help with BIM workflows because they need to stay flexible to handle different project types without creating a completely new process every time.

Video 5: FLOW Waterjet Cutting

Video 5 talks about the FLOW waterject cutting technology and how it's used in advanced manufacturing to improve precision cutting. The main engineering goal talked about in this video was to cut different materials without causing heat damage that happens with other cutting methods. Waterjet systems use high pressure water mixed with abrasive particles to cut materials while keeping their structure the same. This method helped reduce errors and material waste. There are also other cutting methods like laser cutting, plasma cutting, mechanical sawing, and EDM wire cutting that are stil used in manufacturing which work well it can still create heat affected zones that may damage heat sensitive materials One soltuion that helps in advanced manufacturing is using waterjet cutting for materials that require high precision without thermal distortion. Testing and validation could be cutting test samples and measuring dimensional accuracy using coordinate measuring equipment while also comparing surface roughness values to the required specifications. In D&V this could help to use the best tool in a certain task like knowing how different BIM software is used.

Video 6: Ford Rouge Body Manufacturing

Video 6 talks about automated vehicle body production at the Ford Rouge manufacturing facility. The main engineering issue talked about in the video was how robotics, welding systems and material handling processes work together to produce vehicle parts efficiently while being able to meet the safety and quality standards. Engineers have to analyze production flow, cycle time, and equipment performance so that it runs smoothly and avoids delays. Sensors and real time data monitoring help detect problems early which improves reliability and efficiency. One solutuion that could help would be integrating robotic welding systems, automated press operations, and real time dimensional measurement systems throughout the manufacturing process. Sensors and real time monitoring systems can help engineers detect problems earlier and improve reliability and efficiency. Testing and validation could involve body dimensional analysis where assemblies are measured before moving to the next production stage, along with weld quality testing on sample assemblies. The automated manufacturing approach used at Ford Rouge is better than relying heavily on manual assembly because it improves consistency, production speed, and overall quality control during high volume manufacturing. In D&V it would help how large projects move by making it have a quality checks before each phase like how the vehicle bodies are inspected before moving through productuion.

D&V Company Setup and Sustainable Green Focus

D&V Civil Engineering Management was set up to combine important parts in the civil engineering market that most smaller firms offer seperatly like CAD drafting or BIM coordination. The company setup followed the approach covered in the EM-1 materials. The first step was clearly defining the value proposition and scope. D&V focuses on civil engineering projects where BIM coordination and CAD accuracy are critical to project success. The second step was analyzing the competition and identifying what D&V does differently. The third step was building the team structure using a matrix organizational model where team members have both technical discipline accountability and project accountability at the same time. Setting realistic goals for D&V so it follows the 12 rules from the EM-1 goals. The Eisenhower Matrix from the EM-1 is applies to in how D&V prioritizes tasks when multiple projects are running at the same time. D&V's sustainable green focus is built into how every project is evaluated by applying ISO 14001 principles means reviewing the environmental impact of material specifications, recommending lower embodied carbon alternatives where possible, and tracking the environmental performance of design decisions across the project lifecycle. Green building certification support including LEED documentation is a core service because sustainable design is not an add-on for D&V, it is the standard way projects are run.

4-Company Collaboration Plan

D&V plans to collaborate with four types of companies throughout project delivery to provide integrated engineering management services. The first is a structural engineering firm to work on any BIM coordination project the structural model is the backbone that everything else coordinates against. D&V works with the structural engineer's Revit model to run clash detection against mechanical, electrical, and architectural elements. The collaboration means sharing models on a defined update schedule, using agreed file naming conventions, and meeting weekly during active coordination phases. The second is an MEP engineering firm covering mechanical, electrical, and plumbing design. MEP systems create the most coordination conflicts on construction projects. D&V works directly with MEP engineers to resolve clashes early in the design process before they become field problems. This collaboration uses federated BIM models where all disciplines contribute to a shared coordination environment. The third is a general contractor on Construction coordination, where BIM value gets realized. D&V will work with the GC to make sure the coordinated BIM model reflects actual constructability, that the construction sequence makes sense for the site conditions, and that any field changes get captured back in the model so the as-built record stays accurate. The fourth is a sustainability consultant. D&V's green design focus means working closely with sustainability specialists to verify that design decisions meet LEED, WELL, or other certification requirements. The sustainability consultant reviews material specifications, energy modeling inputs, and site design decisions against the certification criteria throughout the project.
